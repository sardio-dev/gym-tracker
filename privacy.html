Privacy

Overload works without an account, and then what you log stays on your device. If you sign in, a copy is kept in your account, and the Social tab shows other people only what you choose.

Without an account

Your workouts, weights, body measurements, nutrition and sleep entries, notes and settings are stored on your device only: in the app's storage on the phone, or in your browser's storage when you use the web app. Nothing of it is sent to me or to anyone else. I cannot see it, and I cannot restore it for you.

  • Backups are files on your device. The automatic weekly backup is written to the app's own folder, and an export goes wherever you send it from the share sheet.
  • Reminders and the rest timer are local notifications, scheduled on the device. The app uses no push service.
  • Apple Health (iPhone) and Health Connect (Android), only if you switch it on in More › Settings: the app saves your timed workouts and your weigh-ins to Health or Health Connect and reads your bodyweight from it. This happens on the device, between the app and the health store. A weigh-in that comes into the app's bodyweight log this way is then part of your data like one you typed: without an account it stays on the device, with an account it is in your account's copy (below). It is never used for ads and never passed to anyone else. Turn it off in the app, or under Health › Sharing › Apps on the iPhone and Health Connect › App permissions on Android.
  • No tracking, no third-party analytics and no ads.

With an account

An account is optional. You sign in with Apple (iPhone) or Google, or try the Social tab as a guest. The account lives in a database run for the app by Supabase (Supabase Inc.), on servers in Frankfurt, Germany. It holds:

  • How you signed in. Apple or Google confirm who you are and pass on an ID and your email address (Apple lets you hide it). The app never sees your password. A guest gets a random ID and nothing else.
  • Your profile. The name and username you choose, a photo if you add one, your home gym, and what the app works out from your training: the weekly score, the level, the weeks on plan and the awards.
  • A copy of your training data (not for guests): workouts, the plan and its settings, nutrition, sleep, weigh-ins and body measurements, and the profile photo. It is written a few seconds after you change something, so that a new phone gets everything back when you sign in. Only your own account can read it.
  • What you do in the Social tab. Check-ins (the gym, whether you are there or going, and the hour), favourite gyms, the week's quests, and the people you blocked or reported.

What other people see. Your name, username, photo, score, level, weeks on plan and awards, on the boards and on your profile. At a gym, your name is on the list of who is there or going only while Who sees my check-ins says Everyone; set it to Nobody under Social › You and you count as a number only. Others see the hour of a check-in, never the minute. Nobody else sees your workouts, weights, body measurements, nutrition, sleep or email address.

I use this data to run the account and the Social tab you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), and for nothing else: no advertising, no profiling, no sale. The body data inside your copy is kept because you signed in to have it kept (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR); deleting the account withdraws that.

Location and the map

The Social tab asks for your location to show the gyms around you. It is optional and only used while the app is open; without it the map starts at a fixed place and you search by name.

  • Your position goes to the app's database to find the gyms near it. It is not stored with your account; a check-in stores the gym, not where you stood.
  • The map's tiles come from OpenFreeMap, and gyms and places the app does not know yet are looked up at OpenStreetMap's public Overpass servers and the Photon search service (Komoot). They receive the area you look at or the words you search for and, as with any internet request, your IP address. They do not learn who you are.

Other connections the app makes

  • Update check (phone app). When it starts, the app asks Expo's update service (Expo, 650 Industries Inc., USA) whether a newer version of the app's own code is available, and downloads it if so. The request carries technical details only: the platform, the app's version, and a random installation ID that is created on the device and is not derived from your hardware, your account or anything you enter. As with any internet request, Expo's servers see your IP address. None of your entries are part of it. The purpose is to deliver fixes (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Without a connection the app simply starts as it is.
  • Pro. A purchase is handled by Apple's App Store or Google Play. The app learns only whether Pro is active; it never sees your payment details.
  • Ads. The app shows none and asks for none. The Android app still contains Google's ad component (AdMob), switched off; when the phone starts the app, that component can send Google technical details such as the phone's ad ID (which you can reset or delete in the phone's settings), the device model and the app's version. None of your entries are sent. Google's policy: policies.google.com/privacy.
  • Web app. gym.sardio.dev is served by GitHub Pages (GitHub Inc., USA), which processes the IP addresses of visitors to deliver the site and keep it secure. After the first visit the app's files are kept in your browser and it works without a connection.

Delete your account

In the app: More › Account › Delete account. This erases the account at once and for good: the sign-in, the profile and photo, the check-ins, scores and awards, and the copy of your training data. What is on your phone stays there until you erase it too (below).

If you cannot open the app, write to hello@sardio.dev from the email address of the account and I delete it within 30 days. Nothing is kept afterwards.

Your control

On the device, everything is in your hands inside the app: More › Backup & data exports all of it as one file, and Delete all data in the same place erases it. Deleting the app, or the website's data in your browser, removes it as well.

For what an account holds you have the rights the GDPR gives you: to see it, to have it corrected or deleted, to take it with you, and to complain to a data protection authority. The export and Delete account cover most of that without asking; for anything else write to me. Without an account I hold no data about you, and if you write to me I only have your message.

Health information

The numbers the app suggests – weights, calories, protein, body fat estimates – are general training guidance worked out on your device from what you entered. They are not medical advice.
